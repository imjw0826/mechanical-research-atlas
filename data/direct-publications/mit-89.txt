Motto

Ab homine ad machinam,
a machina ad hominem.

(From man to machine,
from machine to man.)

Introduction

More than half a century ago, Richard Bellman identified the curse of dimensionality: as system complexity grows, the space of possible states expands exponentially, making control intractable. Humans overcome this limit every day, coordinating over 200 degrees of freedom and 600+ muscles through a neuromotor system that is slow, noisy, and delayed, yet capable of movements that are versatile, robust, and adaptable.

This human motor control paradox sits at the heart of our research. We study how the brain and body resolve this paradox through synergies, impedance regulation, and structured coordination. We translate these principles into the design, control, and clinical validation of assistive and rehabilitative robotic systems. By decoding human movement and embedding its principles into robotic and AI architectures, we aim to build machines that are not only capable, but generalizable, explainable, and truly human-centered. We aim to advance personalized technologies that assist, restore, and augment movement-related quality of life.

The name, Alma, draws on its Latin root almus — “nourishing,” “life-giving” — and its meaning in Spanish as soul. It reflects my conviction that understanding the animating principles of human movement is the key to building machines that move, assist, and “heal” with the same elegance as the body itself. This philosophy is captured in the motto: Ab homine ad machinam, a machina ad hominem — from man to machine, from machine to man. Our research moves in both directions: learning from human coordination to build better machines, and, in turn, using those machines to restore and enhance human movement.
