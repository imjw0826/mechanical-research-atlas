Just Do it Lab

of Additive Manufacturing & Semiconductor Convergence Process

Welcome to JDL

The JDL@KAIST conducts interdisciplinary research integrating artificial intelligence, novel materials, process control, optimal design, and dynamics. By combining advanced manufacturing technologies, computational modeling, and experimental validation, we aim to develop innovative engineering solutions that enhance the performance, intelligence, and reliability of next-generation mechanical systems.



Research Areas
SMP, Metal (PBF/DED), Ceramic 3DP

Advanced Functional Materials for Next-Generation Manufacturing

We develop advanced functional materials to enhance mechanical performance and enable new functionalities. Our research focuses on nanocomposites, smart polymers, and ceramics for advanced manufacturing applications.




1) Nanocomposite Materials

- Development of CNC- and CNT-reinforced polymers for enhanced mechanical properties.

- Optimization of nanofiller dispersion and material composition for additive manufacturing.




2) Smart and Functional Polymers (4DP with SMP)

- Development of shape memory polymers (SMP) for 4D printing applications.

- Investigation of thermomechanical behavior and shape recovery characteristics.




3) Advanced Ceramic & Metal (PBF, DED) 3DP

- Development of ceramic materials for vat photopolymerization 3D printing.

- Optimization of material formulations to improve printability and mechanical performance.

- Potential application: Smart MRO coatings for aerospace and K9 components. 

AI-assisted Optimal Design

Performance-Driven Design of Engineering Systems

We develop advanced design methodologies to improve the mechanical performance and functionality of engineering systems through numerical analysis and optimization. 3D printing enables complex, customized designs that are difficult to manufacture using conventional methods.




1) Patient-Specific Implant Optimization

  - Multi-objective optimization of screw configurations using NSGA-II to minimize stress concentration while reducing stress shielding.

  - Simultaneous optimization of five screw design parameters: position, orientation, diameter, type, and number.

  - Flexible optimization of screw positions and orientations within patient-specific anatomical constraints, without relying on predefined screw holes.




2) Design and Mechanical Characterization of Lattice structure

  - Metal powder bed fusion fabrication of Ti6Al4V lattice (metamaterial) structures with controlled geometric parameters and manufacturing process parameters.

  - Integration of experimental characterization and finite element analysis to investigate mechanical properties and failure mechanisms.

  - Establishment of practical design considerations for lightweight, load-bearing structures based on their mechanical requirements.

3DP Process Control

Intelligent Monitoring and Control for Advanced Manufacturing

We develop intelligent monitoring and control strategies to improve manufacturing precision, quality, and reliability. Our research integrates real-time process monitoring, feedback control, and process optimization for additive manufacturing.




1) Real-Time Extrusion Control

- Camera-based monitoring and measurement of filament diameter during 3D printing.

- Real-time G-code correction for adaptive extrusion control and defect reduction.




2) Process Parameter Optimization

- Investigation of process–property relationships to identify optimal printing conditions.

- Optimization of manufacturing parameters to enhance dimensional accuracy and mechanical performance.




3) Intelligent Process Monitoring

- Development of vision-based monitoring systems for additive manufacturing.

- Data-driven defect detection and quality prediction for improved process reliability.




4) In-Process Structural Dynamics and Control

- Modeling and identification of time-varying and spatially distributed structural dynamics during additive manufacturing.

- Development of feed-forward and feedback control strategies to regulate structural vibration and deformation for improved manufacturing precision and surface quality.



Manufacturing Foundation Model

Data-Driven Intelligence for Advanced Engineering

We develop AI-driven approaches to improve prediction accuracy, quality assessment, and decision-making in engineering systems. Our research integrates machine learning, deep learning, and computer vision for intelligent manufacturing and performance prediction.




1) AI-Based Quality Monitoring

- Development of deep learning models for automated defect detection and quality assessment.

- Application of computer vision and Vision Transformers to additive manufacturing processes.




2) Data-Driven Performance Prediction

- Development of machine learning models to predict material properties and system performance.

- Integration of experimental data and predictive modeling to identify complex relationships between input parameters and performance.




3) Intelligent Modeling and Optimization

- Development of data-driven models for complex engineering systems.

- Application of AI-based prediction and optimization to improve reliability and engineering performance.

Dynamics for Defense Applications

Nonlinear Dynamics for Vibration and Motion Control

We investigate the dynamic behavior of mechanical systems to improve stability, vibration isolation, and motion control. Our research integrates nonlinear dynamics, mechanical modeling, and experimental validation to develop innovative mechanical systems.




1) Nonlinear Vibration Isolation

- Development of bistable and nonlinear stiffness mechanisms for vibration and shock isolation.

- Investigation of dynamic responses to enhance stability and impact protection.




2) Swashplateless Rotor Systems

- Development of swashplateless rotor mechanisms for simplified thrust vector control.

- Investigation of rotor dynamics and control strategies to improve maneuverability and system performance.




3) Dynamic Modeling and Analysis

- Development of analytical and numerical models to predict complex mechanical behavior.

- Experimental characterization and validation of dynamic responses under various operating conditions.

Top 5 Representative Publications 📖
IF 13.2   Predicting Curing Depth in CNT-Resin 3D Printing

Taehyub Lee et al., Prediction of curing depth dependence on CNT nanofiller dispersion for vat photopolymerization 3D printing, Chemical Engineering Journal, 2024. 🔎 

 Carbon nanotubes (CNTs) can add useful properties to photopolymer resins, but they also absorb and scatter UV light, affecting how deeply each printed layer cures. Because curing depth depends on both CNT concentration and dispersion, finding suitable printing conditions can require extensive testing.

 This study presents a model based on a modified Beer–Lambert law to predict curing depth from those two factors. It requires only two spectroscopy measurements and one curing test. The results show that curing depth decreases as CNT concentration increases and as the CNTs become more evenly dispersed. This approach could reduce trial-and-error in setting print parameters and be adapted to other light-curable resin composites.

IF 11.6   Vision Transformers for Printing Quality Inspection 

H. Zhang et al., LLM-Inspired Vision Transformer Framework for Intelligent Quality Recognition in Aerosol Jet Printing, Engineering, 2026. 🔎 

 Aerosol jet printing can produce fine electronic features, but changes in printing conditions and ink properties can lead to defects. This study applies a Vision Transformer (ViT), which uses an attention mechanism inspired by large language models, to recognize printing quality from images. The model first learns visual patterns from about 46,000 unlabeled images, then is fine-tuned using 9,369 labeled images.

 The resulting system classified printing quality with 98.1% accuracy. By learning from both unlabeled and labeled data, the approach supports more reliable defect detection and could help automate quality inspection across material deposition processes.

IF 11.1   Controlling Pores Through Debinding

I. Kim and Y.-J. Yoon, Digital light processing 3D printing of porous ceramics: A systematic analysis from a debinding perspective, Additive Manufacturing, 2024 🔎 

 Porous ceramics are useful in applications such as filters, sensors, and tissue engineering, but their performance depends on the size and distribution of their pores. This study used DLP 3D printing to fabricate PZT ceramic parts containing PMMA particles, which act as pore-forming agents. The researchers then compared different vacuum debinding heating rates to find conditions that remove the organic materials while preserving the desired pore structure.

 A heating rate of 1 °C/min produced crack-free ceramics with low shrinkage and surface roughness. The resulting pores also closely matched the size distribution of the PMMA particles, offering a practical guideline for controlling pore structure in DLP-printed ceramics.

IF 11.1   Linking Print Settings to Material Strength 

J. Lee et al., Average-Accumulated Normalized Dose (A-AND) predicts ultimate tensile strength and elastic modulus of photopolymer printed by vat photopolymerization, Additive Manufacturing, 2022 🔎 

 In vat photopolymerization, a printed part’s strength and stiffness depend on how light cures the resin. Because several printing settings and resin properties affect curing at once, finding the right combination can require many experiments.

 This study introduces Average-Accumulated Normalized Dose (A-AND), a single measure that combines key light exposure and resin-curing factors. A-AND predicted tensile strength and elastic modulus more accurately than conventional measures based on overcuring and layer thickness. The model could help reduce print-parameter testing and material waste.

IF 11.1   Hot Rolling for Stronger 3D-Printed Parts 

A. Andreu et al., Hybrid material extrusion 3D printing to strengthen interlayer adhesion through hot rolling, Additive Manufacturing, 2022 🔎 

Fused deposition modeling (FDM) parts are often weaker between layers because the deposited material cools before it can bond fully. This study adds a heated roller to an FDM printer. After each layer is deposited, the roller presses and reheats it, increasing contact between layers and helping the polymer chains bond.

 Depending on the roller settings, tensile strength increased by up to 38.8%, while scans showed reduced porosity and improved geometric accuracy. The results show how a simple in-process modification can strengthen printed parts without changing the feedstock material.
