Biography

Dr. Punit Kumar is an Assistant Professor in the School of Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering at Nanyang Technological University (NTU), Singapore. His research group focuses on developing advanced structural and functional materials for extreme environments, addressing challenges in affordable, clean energy for sustainable development and advancing humanity’s reach beyond Earth. The group discovers materials for aerospace, hypersonic, nuclear, and green hydrogen applications, requiring lightweight materials that can withstand extreme temperatures (4 K–2273 K) and corrosive, radiation-intensive environments. To accelerate the discovery of such damage-tolerant materials, his team integrates advanced manufacturing techniques, fracture mechanics, and data-driven approaches (AI/ML). His lab will be equipped to experimentally evaluate damage tolerance in extreme environments and will incorporate additive and metamorphic manufacturing capabilities for microstructural design. In addition, Dr. Kumar’s group is interested in functional materials under coupled electro-thermo-mechanical stresses, including those that enable transformative technologies ranging from flexible electronics to resilient electronic systems for extreme environments. Given the multidimensional scope of this research, the group collaborates closely with data science experts in Singapore and with researchers worldwide, while leveraging NTU’s state-of-the-art processing and characterization facilities.

Before joining NTU, Dr. Kumar was a Project Scientist at the University of California, Berkeley, and a Scientist at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. He earned his Ph.D. in Materials Engineering from the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bangalore, where his research received the Acta Student Award in 2019. He has published more than 50 peer-reviewed articles and delivered keynote presentations at major conferences, including the International Conference on Fracture (Atlanta, 2023). Dr. Kumar received the Singapore National Research Foundation (NRF) Fellowship in 2026 and was named among MIT Technology Review’s Innovators Under 35 Asia Pacific the same year. Elsevier/Stanford also recognized him as one of the world’s top 2% of scientists (2023-2026).
