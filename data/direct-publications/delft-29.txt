Biografie

Dingena Schott  is Full Professor of Machine Cargo Interaction Engineering and head of the section Machines & Materials Interactions in the Department of Maritime and Transport Technology, Faculty of Mechanical Engineering, TU Delft.

Schott graduated in Mechanical Engineering at TU Delft in 1999 and received her PhD there in 2004 for research on the homogenisation of bulk materials in mammoth silos. Her work centres on how granular materials behave and how they interact with the equipment that handles, transports and processes them. In 2007 she founded GranChaMlab@TUDelft, where her team characterises, models, calibrates and validates granular materials to enable simulation-supported design of industrial-scale equipment. She was appointed full professor in April 2023 and gave her inaugural address, Advancing Transport and Processing: from Model to Impact, in November 2024.

Her group has made widely used contributions to the Discrete Element Method (DEM), including work on DEM speed-up, particle upscaling for large-scale equipment, co-simulation of DEM with multibody dynamics, and automated calibration frameworks based on genetic and optimisation algorithms. This led to concrete industrial impact, such as an award-winning grab design, developed with Nemag, that unloads vessels faster while being lighter.

Her current research focuses on making particle-based models faster and more useful in practice: machine-learning surrogates and metamodels for granular dynamics, robust design optimisation, and models that can act as digital twins for design and process control. Application areas include energy-transition-driven materials handling (biomass, wood powders, sodiumborohydride NaBH4), segregation and burden behaviour in iron- and steelmaking (blast furnace charging, electric smelting furnaces), the role of particle shape in flowability, and data-driven performance benchmarking of dry bulk terminals.

She also brings her expertise in rock–structure interaction to offshore wind. In the joint industry projects OPIS (Optimising Pile Installation through Scour Protection), carried out with Deltares and industry partners, her group develops high-fidelity DEM models of monopiles penetrating single- and double-layer rock scour protection, complementing large laboratory test campaigns. Together with computational design colleagues, she has also co-developed a metamaterial-based interface that shields impact-driven monopiles from structural resonance, reducing underwater noise during pile driving.

Schott is Chair of CHoPS 2027, the 12th International Conference on Conveying and Handling of Particulate Solids, hosted by TU Delft. She is a board member of DEWIS, the TU Delft network that connects and supports female scientists, and serves as one of TU Delft’s confidential advisors for scientific (research) integrity. She has also served as Director of Studies of the interfaculty MSc programme Transport, Infrastructure and Logistics and led the FAME initiative that repositioned research within the Faculty of Mechanical Engineering.
