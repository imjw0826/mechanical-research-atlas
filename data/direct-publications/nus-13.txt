Welcome to the Sustainable and Multifunctional Metamaterials for Industrial Technologies (SUMMIT) Research Group!
About Us



The SUMMIT Research Group at the National University of Singapore (NUS) is interested in developing sustainable, robust, and multifunctional metamaterial systems to solve global challenges. Our research takes an interdisciplinary approach that bridges mechanics, materials science, advanced manufacturing, and electromechanical systems, combining materials innovation with mechanics-driven design to create multifunctional systems that retain high performance under extreme conditions. These efforts span the full innovation pipeline, from AI-driven metamaterial design exploiting fundamental mechanics and materials science, to scalable fabrication, multiscale characterization, and integration into functional systems, including applications relevant to microelectronics, sensing, and energy technologies.

Recent Research Updates
Double-network-inspired mechanical metamaterials - Nature Materials
Inspired by the entangled structure of double-network hydrogels, the authors integrate stiff truss and compliant woven components into metamaterial architectures to realize simultaneous high stiffness and high stretchability.
Enabling three-dimensional architected materials across length scales and timescales - Nature Materials
Architected materials provide a pathway to achieve properties beyond those of monolithic materials. This Perspective discusses complex architecture designs and their fabrication, characterization and functions across length scales and timescales.
Exploiting multiscale dynamic toughening in multicomponent alloy metamaterials for extreme impact mitigation
A strategy achieves ultrahigh impact mitigation by combining shell microarchitecture with 3D printed multicomponent alloy.
Harnessing screw dislocations in shell-lattice metamaterials for efficient, stable electrocatalysts - Nature Communications
Developing highly active and robust catalysts remains a challenge for the implementation of nitrate reduction. The authors introduce a screw dislocation-mediated 3D printing strategy to fabricate curvature-controlled electrodes with 3D lattice strain, enhancing nitrate reduction performance.
