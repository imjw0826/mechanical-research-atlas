BIO

I am an Assistant Professor based at the Non-Destructive Evaluation Group in the Department of Mechanical Engineering. My research is mainly on ultrasonic material characterisation: I develop tools (both theoretical and experimental) using ultrasound to extract critical structural information within important materials, such as engine alloys and batteries.

1. Ultrasonic Characterisation of Aero-Engine Materials
A primary focus of my research is the use of ultrasound to inspect and characterise key microstructures within critical materials for aero-engines, such as nickel superalloys and titanium alloys. My objective is to create safer, more energy-efficient, and durable metallic parts through ongoing characterisation during manufacturing. My work includes detecting defects like cracks and inclusions and characterising crystallographic texture in components with complex geometries.

2. Non-Destructive Evaluation of Li-ion Batteries
I also spearhead research into the NDE of the Li-ion battery life cycle, aiming for more efficient manufacturing and powerful cell diagnostics. My interests in this area include: a). Inline ultrasonic monitoring of battery slurry, coating, and drying processes. b).Advanced ultrasonic characterisation of battery cell states and conditions. c). Broader NDE applications to speed up electrolyte wetting and develop innovative acoustic and thermal metamaterials for batteries.

3. I am also keenly exploring the opportunities for broarder ultrasonic NDE applications, e.g. on the ultrasonic and optical characterisation of additively manufactured parts and guided ultrasonic measurement of oil film thickness in journal bearings.
