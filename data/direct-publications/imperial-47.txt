BIO

Dr Antonis Sergis is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Mechanical Engineering at Imperial College London and a member of the Energy Storage & Conversion (Multiphysics) section.

 

His research focuses on Advanced Heat Transfer and Storage: understanding, controlling and exploiting the movement and storage of thermal energy to enable cleaner, safer and more efficient technologies. His work brings together fundamental thermofluid science, advanced experimentation, optical diagnostics and computational modelling to address thermal challenges across energy, transport, the built environment and industry.

 

Antonis’s research is organised around two connected themes. The first is advanced heat transfer and thermal management, encompassing boiling, multiphase flow, nanofluids, engineered and self-modifying surfaces, high-heat-flux cooling, nuclear thermohydraulics and advanced flow diagnostics. The second is thermal energy storage, with particular emphasis on phase-change-material systems for inter-seasonal storage, low-grade and waste-heat recovery, building thermoregulation, sustainable cooling and battery thermal management.

 

His approach combines purpose-built experiments with high-speed imaging, laser-based measurements, quantitative image analysis, numerical simulation and molecular modelling. By connecting physical processes across scales, from nanoscale thermal transport and individual bubble dynamics to integrated energy systems, he aims to translate advanced thermal science into practical technologies.

 

Current and developing applications include nuclear fission and fusion, batteries, low-carbon buildings, high-performance heat exchangers, liquid and supercritical carbon dioxide systems, microelectronics, industrial waste-heat recovery and the preservation of temperature-sensitive artefact collections. His research also encompasses uncertainty quantification and the experimental validation of surrogate models and digital twins for nuclear systems.

 

Antonis works closely with academic and industrial partners. His activities include collaborations and research development with the UK Atomic Energy Authority, Rolls-Royce SMR, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Huawei, ILK Dresden and the Victoria and Albert Museum. 

 

He has presented his work to academic, industrial and public audiences, including invited keynote lectures on decarbonising heat through phase-change materials and advanced thermal management.

 

Antonis studied Aerothermal and Aerospace Engineering at the University of Cambridge before completing a PhD in Mechanical Engineering at Imperial College London. Alongside his research, he teaches thermodynamics and heat transfer and supervises research across thermal-energy storage, advanced thermal management, nuclear thermohydraulics and multiphase flows.

 

He welcomes interdisciplinary collaboration with researchers, industry and other organisations seeking to address demanding thermal challenges through fundamental science, advanced diagnostics and engineering innovation.
